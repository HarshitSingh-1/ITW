Part 1: The 4 Big Building Blocks

How It Fits Together

Before diving into code files, here is the bird's-eye view of the 4 tools powering the platform:

🌐 1. The Website (Frontend)

The buttons, cards, colors, and forms that the user sees and clicks on their phone or laptop. Built with clean HTML, Tailwind CSS, and plain JavaScript.

🗄️ 2. Supabase (The Cloud Database)

Think of Supabase as an online, ultra-secure Excel spreadsheet in the cloud where user accounts, event details, ticket numbers, and gatekeeper volunteer lists are stored.

💳 3. Razorpay (The Bank Cashier)

The official payment popup that opens up to collect money securely through UPI (GPay, PhonePe, Paytm), cards, or NetBanking.

🔐 4. Serverless Helpers (api/ folder)

A small private room on the server that keeps our private Razorpay Secret Key safe. It verifies that payment receipts are genuine and triggers instant refunds if seats get full.

💡 The Simple Big Picture
The student clicks a button on the Website $\rightarrow$ Razorpay collects the money $\rightarrow$ our Serverless Helper verifies the receipt $\rightarrow$ Supabase saves the ticket in their account.

Part 2: What, How, Where & When (Step-by-Step)

The Complete User Journey

Step 1: Student Opens the Website & Logs In

app.js
WHEN:
The moment someone opens the website in their phone or laptop browser.
WHAT:
The website checks whether the user is already logged in or if they are a new visitor.
WHERE:
app.js (runs automatically on every single page).
HOW:
app.js listens for Supabase login events. If logged in, it pulls their Name, Roll Number, and College, displays their email in the top navigation bar, and sets up Dark Mode if their phone prefers it.

Step 2: Browsing & Instant Search / Filter

index.html (filterEvents)
WHEN:
The student is on the Home page looking for workshops, sports, or fests.
WHAT:
Typing in the search box or clicking category buttons updates the screen instantly with 0ms delay.
WHERE:
index.html inside the filterEvents() JavaScript function.
HOW:
All events are downloaded from the database once when the page loads. When you type or filter, your browser simply hides cards that don't match. Because your own device does this locally, it feels instantaneous.

Step 3: Booking a Ticket (Money & Seats)

index.html + api/create-order.js
WHEN:
The student clicks "Book Ticket" on a paid or free event.
WHAT:
Collects payment securely and subtracts 1 from the available seats.
WHERE:
index.html triggers api/create-order.js $\rightarrow$ Razorpay popup $\rightarrow$ api/verify-payment.js.
HOW:
1. Our site asks the server: "Create an official bill for ₹100."
2. Razorpay opens on screen and takes UPI payment.
3. Razorpay gives the student a digital receipt.
4. The server mathematically verifies the receipt so nobody can fake it.
5. Supabase saves the new ticket and reduces available seats by 1.

Step 4: The Double-Booking & Instant Auto-Refund Magic

book_ticket_safe + api/refund-payment.js
WHEN:
Only 1 ticket is left, and two students click book at the exact same millisecond.
WHAT:
Student A gets the ticket. Student B gets their money refunded automatically in seconds.
WHERE:
Database function (book_ticket_safe) and api/refund-payment.js.
HOW:
1. Both students pay on Razorpay.
2. The database acts like a locked turnstile: it lets Student A in first, gives them the ticket, and sets seats to 0.
3. When Student B arrives a fraction of a second later, the database says: "SOLD OUT!"
4. Our website immediately catches this error and calls Razorpay's Refund API.
5. Razorpay reverses the money back to Student B's UPI/bank account, and a friendly modal pops up explaining the instant refund.

Step 5: Viewing the Ticket in the Wallet

wallet.html
WHEN:
The student clicks "My Wallet" to view their pass.
WHAT:
Shows a movie-style perforated ticket stub with a secure QR code and calendar link.
WHERE:
wallet.html
HOW:
• Perforated Cutouts: Clean CSS borders that make it look like a physical tearable paper stub.
• The QR Code: Does not store personal details. It only stores a random 36-character unique ID. Nobody can Photoshop or fake it.
• Save as PNG: Clicking "Download Pass" uses html2canvas to take a crisp photo of the ticket so students can save it even without internet.

Step 6: Gate Check-in (Volunteer Camera Scanning)

scanner.html
WHEN:
On event day, at the auditorium or gate entry.
WHAT:
A student volunteer opens the camera scanner on their smartphone to verify entry.
WHERE:
scanner.html
HOW:
1. Uses the phone's back camera directly in the browser (no mobile app installation needed).
2. When a QR is scanned, the scanner pauses for 2 seconds so it doesn't scan repeatedly.
3. If someone shows a forwarded screenshot that was already used: Screen flashes RED $\rightarrow$ "ALREADY USED!"
4. If valid: Screen flashes GREEN with a beep sound $\rightarrow$ Shows Name & Roll No, and marks them present in the database.

Step 7: Participation Certificate (The Lock & Key)

history.html + certificate.js
WHEN:
After the event ends, students visit their History to claim certificates.
WHAT:
Only students who were physically scanned at the door can download their certificate.
WHERE:
Check is in history.html; drawing is in certificate.js.
HOW:
• If the student skipped the event: Button is locked $\rightarrow$ "🔒 Certificate Locked - No Attendance Record".
• If verified present: Clicking download runs certificate.js, which acts like an instant digital artist inside the browser. It draws high-res borders, gold seals, and Dean signatures, and downloads a clean certificate.

Step 8: Crowdfunding (Seniors Festival Donations)

index.html
WHEN:
When clubs or seniors need money for Navratri, farewell, or robot kits.
WHAT:
Displays a campaign goal, how much has been collected, and lets anyone donate any custom amount.
WHERE:
index.html (Donations category & modal).
HOW:
Unlike tickets where the price is fixed (e.g. ₹100), the donation modal lets the user type any amount (₹50, ₹100, ₹500). The progress bar automatically fills up in real time as money comes in.

Step 9: Admin Dashboard & Volunteers

admin.html
WHEN:
When organizers or faculty manage events.
WHAT:
Create events, assign junior volunteers to scan tickets, see charts, and export attendance to Excel.
WHERE:
admin.html
HOW:
• Volunteer Scanner Access: Organizers can type a student's email to make them a "Gatekeeper". That student can scan tickets only for that single event (no admin power).
• Charts: Uses Chart.js to show attendee breakdown by gender, branch, and college year.
• Excel Export: Downloads a clean CSV list of attendees with IST timestamps.

Part 5: Top 6 Viva Questions & Answers

Examiner Cheat-Sheet
Q1: Can someone take a screenshot of their ticket and send it to 5 friends for free entry?
Answer: "No, Sir. The QR code only contains a unique ticket ID. The moment the first student is scanned at the door, the database marks it as 'Already Used'. If a friend tries to scan the same screenshot 2 minutes later, the scanner screen flashes RED with an error sound showing that this ticket was already used."
Q2: How did you prevent double-booking when only 1 ticket is left?
Answer: "We handled this in our Supabase database using a stored procedure called book_ticket_safe. When someone is booking, the database temporarily locks that seat for a fraction of a second to finish the booking before letting the next person in. If someone's payment goes through but the seat was already taken, our system automatically calls Razorpay's API to issue an instant refund."
Q3: Why did you build the QR scanner in the browser instead of making an Android APK?
Answer: "Because college volunteers change for every event. Asking 20 student volunteers to download an APK file takes time and setup. With our web scanner, they just open the website on their phone's Chrome or Safari browser, and it activates their camera immediately with zero installation."
Q4: How do you stop students from editing JavaScript in DevTools to fake a payment?
Answer: "The frontend website never decides if a payment is real. When Razorpay takes money, it generates a cryptographic receipt signature. Our backend helper file (api/verify-payment.js) checks that signature against our secret key on the server. If it doesn't match, the ticket is rejected."
Q5: What makes the Crowdfunding feature different from normal event tickets?
Answer: "Event tickets have a fixed price and limited seats. Crowdfunding campaigns have no seat limits and accept any custom donation amount (e.g. ₹50, ₹100, or ₹500) with a live progress bar showing how close the seniors are to their festival goal."
Q6: Why generate the certificate in the browser instead of on the server?
Answer: "Server-side PDF generators take a lot of server memory and slow down when 500 students download at the same time. Our browser Canvas engine draws the certificate locally on the student's phone in under 80 milliseconds with zero server load and zero cloud costs."
